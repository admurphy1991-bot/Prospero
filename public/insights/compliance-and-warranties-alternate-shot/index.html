Compliance & warranties

Alternate shot: why compliance and warranties have to play as a team.

Two games, one ball. What foursomes golf can teach building owners.

Prospero September 2026 5 min read

In foursomes, you don't get to play your own ball. You hit, your partner hits, and every shot either sets them up or leaves them in the trees. It's the most unforgiving format in team golf, because a partnership is only as strong as its weakest shot.

This week at Medinah, Ryan Fox and Min Woo Lee made it look easy. A Kiwi and an Aussie with two very different games, sharing one ball. They came from behind to beat Collin Morikawa and Wyndham Clark 1 up on the 18th in Friday's foursomes. On Saturday afternoon they did it again, beating Sam Burns and Wyndham Clark. Two days, two foursomes matches, two wins. That helped the Internationals take a 10.5–7.5 lead into the final day.

Watching them, it's hard not to think about buildings. Because building maintenance has its own version of foursomes, and most buildings are playing it badly.

01Two games, one ball

Every commercial building has two sets of maintenance obligations running at the same time.

Compliance is the one everyone knows about. Specified systems such as fire alarms, sprinklers, lifts, emergency lighting and mechanical ventilation have to be inspected and maintained under the building's compliance schedule. Once a year, the owner signs off the Building Warrant of Fitness. It's legally required, it's audited, and nobody forgets it.

Warranty work is the quieter partner. Most manufacturer warranties come with conditions: service intervals, approved technicians, and records to prove it was done. Miss them, and the warranty can be void just when you need it. And when the warranty period ends, the manufacturer's maintenance requirements don't. They're what keeps the asset performing for its full life.

Here's the foursomes part: these aren't two separate games. They're often the same asset, and sometimes the same service visit, done for two reasons at once. One missed visit can cost you twice.

02The partner nobody's watching

In most buildings, compliance and warranties are managed like two players on different courses. Compliance lives with the IQPs and the BWoF paperwork. Warranties live in the O&M manuals, if anyone knows where those are.

That creates two kinds of dropped shot.

The first is the shared asset. The lift contractor does the compliance inspection, but nobody checks whether the manufacturer's own service conditions were met, or whether the records would satisfy a warranty claim. The work might even be done, but without the right evidence it doesn't count.

The second is the asset that isn't on the compliance schedule at all. Roof membranes, cladding, sealants, coatings and joinery often carry long warranties with maintenance conditions attached. Because they're not specified systems, they never show up on the BWoF checklist. Nobody's watching them until water comes through the ceiling, and by then the warranty may already be gone.

And this is where it matters most: these assets don't stop being critical when the warranty runs out. Think of the joint sealants around a window thirty floors up, or a roof that takes sun, wind and driving rain, sometimes all in the same day. Nothing on the compliance schedule requires anyone to check them. But if they fail, the damage, the cost and the risk are just as real in year fifteen as in year two.

None of this means compliance matters less. It means the compliance schedule is only half the scorecard. The best-run buildings balance the two: the checks the law requires, and the checks the building needs.

Compliance keeps you legal. Warranties keep you covered. Balancing both is what keeps the building performing long after the warranty's gone.

04Closing it out

A lead going into the final day is a great position, but it isn't a trophy. The singles still have to be played, one match at a time.

Buildings are the same. A clean BWoF is a good position to be in, but it doesn't mean the building is fully looked after. The warranty obligations, the roofs, façades and sealants that sit outside the compliance schedule, and the evidence behind every service visit all still have to be closed out, year after year, long after the warranties have expired.

The best partnerships make each other better. Put compliance and warranties on the same card, and your building gets the benefit of both.
